HR Analytics Dashboard: Salary Patterns & Workplace Equality

A freelance Power BI dashboard analyzing compensation and workforce composition — built to give a client a clear, evidence-based view of pay and representation patterns across their organization.

Compensation Analysis Workplace Equality Power BI
20+Business Insights Delivered
6Dimensions Analyzed
1Client-Ready Dashboard

Overview

A freelance engagement to analyze workforce salary and demographic data and present the findings as a self-service Power BI dashboard the client's HR team could explore on their own, rather than requesting a new report for every question.

Business Challenge

Analyze salary patterns and workplace equality across the organization specifically, whether pay and career progression were consistent across gender, age, and family circumstances, or whether gaps existed that leadership wasn't aware of.

Dataset

Employee-level records covering gender, age, occupation, marital status, family size, and salary, cleaned and modeled in Power BI to support filtering and drill-down by any combination of these dimensions.

Dashboard Screenshots

Business Questions Answered

  • Are there measurable pay gaps by gender or age within the same occupation?
  • How does family size or marital status correlate with salary or role level?
  • Which occupations show the widest internal pay spread?

Insights

  • Delivered more than twenty distinct business insights spanning gender, age, occupation, marital status, family size, and salary.
  • Structured the dashboard so HR could filter by any dimension without needing a new report built for each question.
  • Framed every insight in terms of a compensation or diversity decision the client could act on, not just a chart.

Recommendations

  • Use the dashboard as a recurring input to annual compensation reviews, not a one-off report.
  • Investigate any occupation showing a wide internal pay spread for role-scoping or leveling issues.
  • Track workforce diversity metrics on the same cadence as compensation, since the two are closely linked.

Technology Used

Power BIExcelDAX

Lessons Learned

Working directly with a client rather than a simulated brief meant the requirements shifted as they saw early drafts the dashboard that shipped answered several questions the client hadn't thought to ask at the start. It taught me to build for exploration, not just for the specific chart a client initially requests.
