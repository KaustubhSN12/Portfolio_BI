Manufacturing Analytics Dashboard: Cutting Downtime Across Factories

A Tableau dashboard built to help plant managers see, at a glance, which factories and which machines were driving the most production downtime and where a preventive maintenance plan would pay off first.

Overview

This project was completed as part of Deloitte's Data Analytics Job Simulation. The brief: manufacturing leadership needed visibility into which factories and machines were causing the most unplanned downtime, so maintenance budget could be targeted rather than spread evenly across every site.

Business Challenge

Identify production downtime across multiple factories, and turn device-health telemetry into a prioritized, business-readable view that non-technical stakeholders could act on without needing to query raw data themselves.

Dataset

Device-level health and failure logs across Daikibo Manufacturing's factory network, including machine type, factory location, and unhealthy-device counts, modeled and shaped using Power Query before being loaded into the Tableau data model.

Dashboard Screenshots

Business Questions Answered

  • Which factories have the highest concentration of unhealthy devices?
  • Which machine types fail most often, across the entire network?
  • Where should preventive maintenance budget be spent first?

Insights

  • Daikibo Factory Seiko had the highest downtime, with 480 devices flagged unhealthy.
  • Daikibo Shenzhen followed closely, with 420 unhealthy devices — together the two sites accounted for the majority of network-wide downtime risk.
  • Laser Welders were the single most failure-prone machine type, with Laser Cutters close behind.

Recommendations

  • Prioritize preventive maintenance schedules at Daikibo Seiko and Daikibo Shenzhen before other sites.
  • Fast-track inspection cycles for Laser Welders and Laser Cutters network-wide.
  • Roll the dashboard out to plant managers directly, so maintenance decisions don't wait on a monthly report.

Technology Used

Tableau DAXPower QueryExcel

Lessons Learned

This was my first time modeling downtime data at a network-wide scale rather than a single site, and it pushed me to think harder about aggregation level a plant manager needs machine-level detail, while an executive needs a single "where's the risk" number. Building both views off one data model, using measures instead of duplicated tables, was the key DAX lesson from this simulation.
